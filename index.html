How it works
  • You start with the same cash either way: the deposit plus buying costs. A buyer spends it on the house; a renter invests it.
  • Each month, both have the same budget: rent plus monthly savings. A renter pays rent and invests the savings; a buyer pays the mortgage and running costs and invests what's left. The savings are the same on both sides, so they don't change who wins, only how much each ends up with.
  • Selling at any point: buyers get the home's value minus the mortgage left and selling costs, plus their investments. Renters have their investments.
  • Break-even is the first month from which buying stays ahead of renting.
  • The mortgage rate is assumed to stay the same for the whole stay. Best 2-year fixes in Oct 2026 were about 4.9% (60% loan-to-value) to 5.3% (90%).
  • Not included: tax on investment gains (assumes an ISA), inflation, and stamp duty changing with price (buying costs are a fixed amount).
